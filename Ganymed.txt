Ganymed is the German form of Ganymede, in mythology the beautiful Trojan boy with whom Zeus fell in love, and carried off to Mount Olympus when he (Zeus) turned himself into an eagle.  (The name Ganymede was first used by Galileo for one of the moons of Jupiter.)  The asteroid Ganymed seems to balance Hebe in energy terms.  She was the original cup-bearer to the gods and goddesses, but Ganymede took her place.  At this stage of research, many of the themes around the asteroid Ganymed seem to be similar to those pertinent to Hebe.  Ganymed is also a pointer to service, or to exploitation by the powerful.  It has a high level of ardour, like Hebe, but the image of the medieval knight errant is perhaps more relevant here, where the young man rides off on adventures geared to the refinement of his soul and the perfection of his worldly skills.
